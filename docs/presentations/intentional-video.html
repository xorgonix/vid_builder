Act I

Why vid-build

The intent gap — and how this studio closes it.

You know what should happen. The tools don’t.

You can picture the shot: it begins here, something changes, it ends there. You know what the audience must notice.

Then you open a tool and all of that collapses into a text box — one prompt, one clip, no memory of the reason it exists.

What gets lost between tools

  • the purpose of the shot
  • the change it must show
  • continuity from the previous cut
  • the ability to reproduce a result
  • the decision trail of who approved what

The turn

vid-build is not a generator and not an editor.
It is the development studio in between — the place your intention lives.

Generation becomes one repeatable step inside a process you can see, review and reproduce.

The intent spine: every shot has a why

Brief · direction→ Section · purpose + budget→ Scene · what changes→ Beat · dramatic unit→ Shot · state A → state B

The rule that makes it intentional

A shot is the smallest unit that begins in one state and ends in another. If you can’t name the visible change, the shot isn’t ready — and the studio says so plainly: “No visible change detected — the shot lacks a payoff.”

Intent, made concrete

First/last-frame conditioning

Approved start and end frames become the anchors of a motion generation. Motion is directed toward a destination, not guessed from adjectives.

No silent start-only fallback: if both endpoints aren’t selected and approved, the request is refused.

motion: conditioned

Continuity as state, not pixels

Each cut stores an inherit / recompose handoff: what persists, what intentionally changes, what is unresolved — with a motivation for the cut.

The next shot’s start state is derived from the previous shot’s end, then you approve it.

world: consistent

Why this matters: “visuals matching your intention” isn’t a vibe — it’s endpoints, continuity and a named change, all enforced by the process.

AI proposes. You apply. Everything leaves a receipt.

Human authority

Planner output arrives as proposals — versioned, field-by-field, and marked stale when the thing it was based on changes. Nothing is silently overwritten.

Reproducible

Workflows are immutable with a SHA-256; attempts record seeds; assets carry checksums. You can explain how any frame was made.

Accountable

Approval is a human decision — including approve-with-exception and a recorded reason. An audit log records the actor. Selection is not approval.

Local-first, private, and it ends in a file

  • Your SQLite database, your media folder.
  • Your LM Studio for the planner, your ComfyUI for pixels.
  • No per-clip cloud rent; your work doesn’t leave the machine.
  • FFmpeg assembles the final MP4 — or a 5/15/30s duration video.

What vid-build is not

It is not for performance, talking heads, lip-sync or “expressive” AI acting. If the video’s value is a human speaking or an artistic flourish, this is the wrong tool.

It is not creativity-in-a-box. It won’t invent your taste; it will hold your decisions and make the visuals obey them.

It is for controlled visual communication — explainers, promos, previs, product and training — where you know what the audience should notice.

Today only a local ComfyUI target executes; rental/API targets can be planned but not yet run. Restore is a CLI tool by design.

Act II

How to use it

Two loops — one free, one paid — then a deliverable.

From a sentence to an exported film

Develop · idea → brief→ Outline · beats → shots→ Block · START·CHANGE·END→ Generate · endpoints → motion→ Review · select → approve→ Edit · assemble → export

Cheap loop first, expensive loop second. Do all the thinking with no GPU. Only cross into generation once the film already works on paper.

Loop 1 · free — decide the film before you pay for pixels

Outline · scene → beat → shot

shotHands and clay · 3.0s · blocking_only

  • Propose beats and shots; untick, edit, apply.

Edit & export

Assemble the cut from approved selections; cuts, fades and dissolves preserve every clip. Export one MP4.

Duration video
