Direct the film.
Don’t babysit the prompt.

A local-first studio that takes one idea to a finished, multi-shot video — and keeps every human decision.

The problem with AI video today

  • An idea lives in your head, a doc, and a dozen prompt tabs.
  • You spend money on pixels before you know the film works.
  • Every generation is a slot machine — and its recipe is gone tomorrow.
  • You can’t answer: which model, which settings, why did I keep this take?

What vid-build is

A studio, not a prompt box

  • Brief → outline → storyboard → production → review → edit
  • Runs on your machine, over your models

You stay the director

  • AI proposes; you apply and approve
  • Nothing expensive or decisive happens silently

The path, left to right

Develop→ Outline→ Board→ Produce→ Review→ Edit→ Compute

The order is increasing commitment. You can move freely; the studio shows what’s actually ready.

Develop — from ambiguity to a decision

  • Write the premise, constraints and open questions.
  • Ask the built-in planner to reflect and refine — it discusses, it doesn’t change your project.
  • Request a brief proposal, then tick only the fields you accept.

Conversation is evidence. The accepted brief is authority.

Outline — decide what the film says

  • Sections, scenes and shots — ordered, reorderable.
  • Every shot carries a purpose and a required visible change.
  • Duration budgets keep the total honest, before any rendering.

Board — see the film before the pixels

Each shot card shows START · CHANGE · END.

  • Blocking is a structured sketch, drawn instantly as SVG — no model call.
  • The end must visibly pay off the start.
  • Readiness badges tell you what’s missing.

Blocking — stage the shot cheaply

Paired editor

  • Start and end side by side
  • Move subjects, gaze, props, camera

Change analysis

  • Flags pose, position, gaze, prop, camera differences
  • Warns when nothing changes

Ask the planner for a blocking proposal if you want a starting point.

Prompt workbench — layers, not spaghetti

  • Separate layers: project style, character, location, motion, experiments.
  • Each layer is inherit / append / replace / suppress / lock.
  • See the exact compiled prompt before anything runs.
  • First-frame and last-frame text are always kept separate.

Produce — spend deliberately

  • Freeze the current specification into a request.
  • Choose candidates, quality tier, seed policy, model profile.
  • Run locally now, or save it for a planned campaign.

Blocking and concepts are cheap. Final renders are a decision, not a default.

Review — choose with evidence

  • Compare full-size candidates with a receipt: model, seed, workflow, time.
  • Select the take you want; approve, reject, or approve with a recorded exception.
  • Automated assessments are advice. The approval is yours.

Edit & export — assemble the cut

  • Order approved clips, trim, choose cut / fade / dissolve.
  • Validate, then export a single MP4 with a receipt linking back to the source assets.

Compute planner — where should this run?

  • Estimates from your measured runs, not marketing numbers.
  • Compare local GPU, rented workers and APIs.
  • Bundle ready work into a campaign, preflight it, launch, then reconcile actual cost.

On your phone

  • Pair a phone or tablet over LAN with a short-lived, single-use code.
  • Review, comment and approve on the couch.
  • Each device has its own revocable session. Disable anytime.

Your local models stay private — the device talks to vid-build, never to ComfyUI.

What it will never do silently

Never

  • Replace your brief or outline
  • Approve or select a take for you
  • Spend on rentals or APIs
  • Overwrite a generation

Always

  • Show what changed and why
  • Keep every attempt as history
  • Ask before expensive work
  • Attribute decisions to you

Getting started

git clone <repo>
cd vid_builder
cp config.example.json config.json
templ generate
go run ./cmd/vidbuild -open

Then sign in and create your first project. That’s it — idea to film, one visible change at a time.

Make the film you can already see.
